Made for shallow pools

Moving the pool won't move your payout.

A small coin is only safe to list if a whale pushing its pool can't push money out of the vault. Every coin price runs through six checks before it touches a position.

01

Worse-side fills

Opening a long fills at the higher of spot and the 10-minute average. Opening a short fills at the lower. A spike never lands in the trader's favor.

02

Drift pause

When spot runs 10% or more away from its average, new positions on that coin wait. Closing and adding margin keep working.

03

Per-side ceilings

Long interest is capped by what it costs to push the pool up. Short interest is capped by what it costs to push it down. Lopsided pools get lopsided limits.

04

Cooling-off

A new listing collects an hour of price history before it accepts its first position.

05

Win ceiling

One coin position can win at most 3× its entry size. That limits what a slow, deliberate pump can take from the vault.

06

Average-price margin

Margin and liquidation checks read the smoothed price, so a single candle can't wipe out a healthy position.

Docs

Short answers.

Is this real?

No. This is a demo on a simulated network. Prices, blocks, fills and the vault are generated in your browser. Connect only turns on a paper wallet.

How is a coin's leverage chosen?

From the dollars needed to move its pool 10%. Under $75K gets 2×, under $250K gets 3×, under $1M gets 4×, and anything deeper gets 5×. Majors and stocks use oracle prices and go to 20×.

Where does the liquidation price come from?

A position closes when its margin, after PnL, drops below 1% of its size. For a long that is fill × (1 − 1/leverage + 0.01). For a short it is fill × (1 + 1/leverage − 0.01).

What does "10% move" mean on a card?

The dollars a trader would have to spend in the pool to move the coin's price by 10%. Bigger is safer and unlocks more leverage.

Where are my demo positions saved?

In this browser's local storage. Reset in the terminal wipes them.
